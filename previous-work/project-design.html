Visitor Management System: workflows and database design

Project Workflow

Two ways a visitor reaches the building. Each diagram is a swimlane: the row says who does the step, and arrows show the order.

1. Expected visitor

HR/Admin registers the visitor before the visit. The receptionist already has them on the Expected list, so arrival is a verify, check-in and card hand-over. Check-out is complete only when the card is back.

StepDecisionOutcomeDeniedDiffers from the PRD (see note)
  1. HR/Admin registers the visitor in advance. The system saves the visit as Expected.
  2. The visitor receives an invitation email. (Requested for this design; see the note below.)
  3. On arrival the receptionist finds the visitor on the Expected list and verifies who they are.
  4. The receptionist selects an Available card and clicks Check-in. The visit becomes Checked In and the card Issued; HR/Admin is notified.
  5. At the end of the visit the visitor returns the card, and the receptionist is asked “Did you get the visitor card back?”
  6. Yes: the visit becomes Checked Out and the card Available. No: the visitor stays Checked In and the card stays Issued until it is recovered.
Not in the PRD: the PRD sends invitation emails only for employee and receptionist accounts. It does not describe an email to the visitor, and the app does not send one. It is drawn here because it was requested, and would need a decision before it is built.

2. Unexpected visitor (walk-in), as requested

The receptionist registers someone who arrived without notice and asks the named employee to approve before letting them in.

StepDecisionOutcomeDeniedDiffers from the PRD (see note)
  1. The receptionist enters the visitor’s name, phone number, purpose and the employee they want to see.
  2. The system emails that employee asking for approval.
  3. The employee approves or rejects.
  4. Approved: the receptionist checks the visitor in and issues a card.
  5. Rejected: the visitor is denied entry.
Conflicts with the PRD. Section 5 (Workflow C) and section 7 say: “Unscheduled visitors do not require additional employee approval after registration” and “Once a visitor entry has been added to the system, no additional employee approval is required for check-in.” The receptionist searches for the host, or records the name HR/Admin confirmed in Invited By, and checks the visitor in straight away. The approval steps above are not built, and the database has no approval table or Rejected status. This diagram shows your requested design, not the current behaviour. If it is wanted, it changes a business rule, so it needs a decision first.
How the PRD describes this workflow (what the app does today)

The receptionist registers the visitor, searches for the employee, records the confirmed name in Invited By when the employee is not found, checks the visitor in, and HR/Admin is notified. No approval step exists.

Database Design

Six tables as requested. PK marks a primary key, FK a foreign key. A line with a bar at one end and a three-pronged foot at the other is a one-to-many relationship: one row on the bar side relates to many rows on the foot side.

PK primary keyFK foreign key| one⋔ many
Added for traceability: visits.registered_by is not in the list you gave. Without it nothing records which HR/Admin or receptionist account entered the visit. Remove it if you do not want it.

What each table is for

users ACCOUNTS

The people who sign in to run the system: HR/Admin and receptionist accounts, with their role and whether the account is active.

employees DIRECTORY

The organization’s staff: name, team and email. A visitor comes to see one employee, who is the host. Receptionists should see only name and team.

visitors PEOPLE

One row per person who visits: name and phone. A returning visitor keeps one row and gets a new visit each time.

visits CORE

One row per occasion: who, whom they see, why, and the status with the expected arrival, check-in and check-out times. Status moves Expected, then Checked In, then Checked Out.

visitor_cards CARDS

The cards given out at check-in, as requested: the card number, the visit it was issued for, and when it was issued and returned. A card with no return time is still out.

approvals APPROVAL

For the walk-in design above: the employee’s decision about a visit, and when they made it. Needed only if employee approval is adopted.

This design and the database that is built

The six tables are the design you asked for. The database the app uses today is close but not the same, and this document does not change it.

Where the built database differs
This designBuilt database
usersprofiles, linked to Supabase Auth. It holds the role (HR/Admin, receptionist or employee) and the active flag. Employees can also sign in.
employeesAdds Employee ID, designation, phone, active, and a can_register_visitors permission. Receptionists read only name and team, through a view.
visitorsAdds a Visitor ID (V-1001…). Name and phone together identify the person.
visitsAdds the visitor type, guest counts, company, Invited By (an employee or a typed name), and who registered it. Status is Expected, Checked In or Checked Out only.
visitor_cardsOnly the card number and its current status (Available or Issued). Issue and return times live in a separate card_assignments table, which keeps the full history and is never deleted.
approvalsNo such table. The PRD says no approval is needed for employee-registered or walk-in visitors. There is no Rejected status.
(not in this design)notifications (check-in alerts for HR/Admin and the registering employee) and app_settings.
